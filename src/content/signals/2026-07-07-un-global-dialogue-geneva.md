**What happened.** The two bodies created by the UN General Assembly in August 2025 have both now begun work. The 40-member Independent International Scientific Panel on AI, appointed in February 2026, released its preliminary report on AI's opportunities, risks, and impacts on 1 July. On 6 and 7 July, the first Global Dialogue on AI Governance met in Geneva, with delegations from more than 160 countries alongside industry, civil society, and academia. The next session is scheduled for May 2027 in New York.

**Why it matters.** The summit series that began at Bletchley involved a relatively small group of countries and drifted in focus. The UN process includes nearly every country, giving the Global South a formal voice in AI governance for the first time, and gives the world a second scientific assessment alongside the International AI Safety Report.

**How to read it.** Apply the first of the policy-reading questions: what kind of instrument is this? Both bodies are advisory. They produce assessments and dialogue, not binding rules. Their influence will depend on whether national governments and other forums take up their findings, so watch for citations of the panel's report in national policy and in the next summits.
