**What happened.** Erik Brynjolfsson, Bharat Chandar, and Ruyu Chen updated their analysis of ADP payroll records. As of June 2026, employment for workers aged 22 to 25 in the most AI-exposed occupations stood about 19% below where it would have been had it kept pace with less-exposed occupations, widening from the gap first documented in 2025. The adjustment continues to come mainly through slower hiring rather than layoffs, concentrates in occupations where AI tends to automate rather than augment, and does not appear for experienced workers.

**Why it matters.** It remains the most-cited evidence that AI is already reshaping entry into the labor market, with direct implications for how early-career workers build skills and for education and training policy. The headline of the update is twofold: the gap is widening, and there is still no sign of economy-wide displacement.

**How to read it.** "19% below its counterfactual" is a relative shortfall against an estimated trend, not a 19% loss of jobs caused by AI. The authors describe the patterns as descriptive rather than causal, and other researchers note pre-existing trends and sample limits. Use the difference-in-differences checklist from the evidence module: check the comparison group, the pre-trends, and the alternative explanations.
