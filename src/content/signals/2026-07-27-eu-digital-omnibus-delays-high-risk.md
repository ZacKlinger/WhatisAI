Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, the Digital Omnibus on AI, was published in the Official Journal on 24 July 2026 and took effect three days later. It is the first substantial amendment to the AI Act, and it changes *when* obligations bite rather than *what* they require.

**What moved.** Obligations for stand-alone high-risk systems listed in Annex III (hiring, credit scoring, education, and similar uses) now apply from 2 December 2027 instead of 2 August 2026. High-risk AI embedded in products already covered by EU safety law (Annex I) moves to 2 August 2028. An earlier draft had tied these dates to the readiness of technical standards; the final text uses fixed dates instead.

**What did not move.** Most Article 50 transparency duties, such as telling people they are talking to an AI, still apply from 2 August 2026. Providers of generative systems already on the market have until 2 December 2026 to mark AI-generated content. New prohibitions on AI used to create non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material apply from 2 December 2026. Obligations for general-purpose AI models, in force since August 2025, are unaffected.

**Why it matters.** A deferral is not a repeal. For a reader of policy, the lesson is to separate a law's *entry into force* from its *application dates*, and to check whether amendments change substance or schedule. Here, it is schedule. Companies building high-risk systems gained roughly sixteen months. Regulators gained time to finish the harmonized standards that make compliance concrete.
