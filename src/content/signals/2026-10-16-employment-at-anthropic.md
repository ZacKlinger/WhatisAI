**What happened.** Anthropic's economics team published a technical report using the company's own headcount, usage logs, output metrics, surveys, and hiring records. Among the findings: the share of junior hires in technical roles fell from about 27% to 9% since early 2025; combined agent work hours passed human work hours in March 2026; code commits per engineer reached about five times their level of six months earlier, with no detectable difference in how often agent-written code was later reverted; and "meta-work," such as building agent tools and monitoring pipelines, rose from 10% to 22% of interactive sessions.

**Why it matters.** It is a rare inside view of a workplace at the leading edge of agent adoption, and its junior-hiring pattern echoes the economy-wide "canaries" evidence on early-career workers in exposed occupations. It also documents the shift the curriculum describes from doing work to directing and checking it: hiring scorecards increasingly emphasize autonomy and judgment over domain expertise.

**How to read it.** Apply the labor-study checklist. This is one unusually AI-intensive company, adoption was not random, the period coincided with rapid business growth, and the authors state the data do not establish that AI caused the junior-hiring decline. Treat it as a vivid, well-measured case study, not a forecast for the economy.
