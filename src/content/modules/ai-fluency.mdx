## A task to carry through the course

Use the official lessons above as your main instruction. They introduce the framework in its creators' own words, with videos, transcripts, and exercises. You can spread this module over several sessions.

For our companion activity, imagine you are selecting a venue for a small community event. You have three public venue pages, a fixed budget, accessibility requirements, and a date. Your goal is a recommendation you can defend with evidence. Any general-purpose AI assistant can help; you can also rehearse the decisions on paper.

## Plan the division of work

Before opening an assistant, list the decisions that require your knowledge of the community. Choose a small piece of work the assistant could usefully attempt, such as organizing published venue information. Decide who will confirm availability and make the final choice.

## Brief, inspect, revise

Write down the columns you need in a comparison and what should happen when a venue page is silent. Ask for source references you can open. Inspect at least two entries against the original pages. If an unsupported claim appears, revise your request and check the revised result again. A second fluent answer is still something to examine.

## Decide whether to use the result

Separate a draft recommendation from permission to contact a venue or make a booking. Check the information you plan to share, record where AI helped, and decide what remains unverified. Keep responsibility for the decision with the person who will act on it.

## Keep the practice going

Carry this record into the agents, evaluation, and workplace-evidence lessons: what you intended, what you delegated, what you asked, what you checked, and what you changed. The four competencies are connected; checking a result often sends you back to change the brief or reconsider the task itself.
